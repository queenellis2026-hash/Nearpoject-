1. The job & the approved contract sum

The homeowner and the crew agreed a contract sum up front. The clerk may never pay past it.

2. Agent clerk prepares the invoice

3. Resolve & human approval

If the clerk flags an overage, a human either corrects the invoice or approves a change order that raises the approved sum — before approval. Once the client approves, the approved sum is frozen; on-chain, only a new invoice can change it. (In this simulated desk, a correction re-uses the same invoice id — the one place the sim differs from the chain.) The clerk then re-checks. Nothing is payable before a human approves.

4. Payment — escrow on NEAR (simulated locally)

Two steps, as on-chain: first the approved invoice is funded into escrow, then the funding client releases it to the crew. The escrow contract enforces the same order on NEAR testnet — funding requires the client's approval first.

6. Live on-chain — connect a testnet wallet and do it for real

This section writes REAL signed transactions to the deployed testnet contract (paid-in-full-v3.testnet). Sections 1–4 above stay a simulation; this section is the real thing. Amount honesty: the live invoice uses a small testnet amount — 0.01 NEAR by default — NOT the $12,000 simulated invoice above. Nothing here moves the $12,000; that invoice never touches the chain.

Connect a NEAR testnet wallet (MyNearWallet). The wallet asks you to approve a function-call key limited to the five contract methods on this contract only — no full-access key, no other contract. You are redirected to the wallet to sign each transaction, then back here. The connected account becomes the recorded client on any invoice it creates — only that client can approve, fund, release or cancel it.

Each step signs a transaction in your wallet and shows the transaction hash (link to the testnet explorer) plus the invoice state read back live from the chain. If you have no testnet wallet, sections 1–5 still demo the whole flow — this section is the live proof for judges who do.
